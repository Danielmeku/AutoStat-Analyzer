# Analysis workflow and methods

The supplied Regression Analysis (Stat3041) lecture notes by Yabebal Ayalew guide this upgrade. They are reference material, not application instructions. Statistical checks remain conditional on their assumptions; a diagnostic passing is not proof that the assumptions hold.

## Workflow

1. Review missingness, duplicate records, variable types, independence, pairing and sampling design. Cleaning is explicit and preserves the original upload.
2. Explore distributions and relationships. Choose inferential tests to match the scientific question; partial correlation adjusts linearly for selected numeric controls. Levene (median-centered) and Bartlett compare variance across independent groups.
3. Specify the regression. Categorical main effects use reference coding with sorted observed levels; numeric transformations apply before quadratic and interaction construction. Interactions retain their main effects. No dataset-provided names are evaluated as code. Complete cases are selected across all model and weight columns.
4. Fit and inspect inference. The selected confidence level controls coefficient and prediction intervals. Results identify the actual estimator, response scale, covariance method, reference categories, terms and excluded rows. Coefficient partial R² uses the classical OLS t statistic conditional on the other expanded terms, even when the displayed uncertainty uses HC3.
5. Review diagnostics. Plots retain original active-dataset row positions. Influence thresholds are review heuristics. No automatic deletion, transformation, feature selection or declaration that the model is valid takes place. Optional sensitivity comparisons exclude only explicitly selected active row positions from a separate fit; the active data and primary result remain unchanged. Box–Cox sensitivity is unavailable because reselecting its power changes the modeled scale. Observation-level diagnostics are available through an explicit CSV download and are excluded from reports and AI context.
6. Apply a justified remedy and refit. HC3 changes coefficient/joint inference, not the fitted mean or the error model. WLS weights must be positive inverse variances and externally justified. Box–Cox maximizes a regression profile likelihood over −3 to 3, rather than optimizing marginal normality of the response. Inference after transformation/model selection is exploratory and conditional on the chosen specification.
7. Predict new cases from the stored fitted specification, with fixed reference categories and transformation parameters. Numeric-range extrapolation is flagged; this does not detect all unsupported combinations. Unseen categories and incomplete/nonfinite inputs are rejected. Mean and individual intervals are distinct. WLS omits individual prediction intervals without a future observation weight. Inverse-transformed estimates are explicitly labelled and are not bias-corrected conditional means; intervals crossing the inverse domain fail with an explanatory message.
8. Validate predictive alternatives with the same holdout. Optional ridge/lasso use a user-chosen positive penalty and fold-local numeric scaling. Their inclusion does not change the baseline/linear/forest splits. Numeric imputation/scaling and category encoding are fitted inside each training partition/fold. No holdout-driven tuning is performed. Analytic OLS PRESS and predicted R² are separate leave-one-out diagnostics, not independent confirmation after selection.
9. Export current results. JSON, ZIP and detailed PDFs include model specification, inference, diagnostic summaries and review suggestions. New-case predictions and row diagnostics have separate explicit CSV downloads. Gemini and the offline guide receive only allowlisted aggregate evidence. Encoded category values, row diagnostics and new-case predictions are excluded. Changing completed evidence clears outdated assistant commentary and PDFs.

## Statistical and implementation limits

- All tests/diagnostic p-values are unadjusted. Classical inference assumes the specified mean/variance model and independent observations. HC3 addresses heteroscedastic coefficient covariance, not clustering or temporal dependence.
- Breusch–Godfrey and Durbin–Watson depend on the existing data order; the app does not infer a time/group design. Arbitrary GLS covariance models, AR model estimation, clustered inference and time/group-aware validation remain outside this upgrade. WLS is the supported diagonal-covariance special case of GLS.
- WLS normality and variance checks use whitened residuals. Ordinary OLS influence, pure-error lack-of-fit, RESET and Breusch–Godfrey are not applied to WLS. OLS lack-of-fit needs replicated predictor settings, positive pure-error variation and both residual components' degrees of freedom. Near-perfect fits suppress unstable residual/influence checks. Shapiro–Wilk, Lilliefors and Anderson–Darling are not run above 5,000 observations. White testing is limited to 120 auxiliary terms. Goldfeld–Quandt requires an explicitly selected numeric ordering predictor and adequate split-group degrees of freedom; it omits the middle fifth and tests two-sided variance change.
- No general claim of unbiasedness or causal identification follows from residual diagnostics. Studentizing residuals does not itself make heteroscedastic inference valid.
- Prediction intervals remain model-based even with HC3. AIC/BIC values from different response transformations are not directly comparable. Data-driven Box–Cox selection is not a held-out optimization or a guarantee of adequate residuals.
- Models allow up to 20 original predictors, 20 levels per inferential categorical predictor and 60 expanded terms. Rank-deficient models and models without sufficient residual degrees of freedom are rejected. Plotting uses bounded samples; fitting and diagnostic calculations use all complete cases. Deleted-residual influence measures use algebraic formulas rather than one refit per row.
